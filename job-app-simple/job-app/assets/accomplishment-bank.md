## [Role or project] — [Org] — [dates]

**What it was:** one or two sentences of context. What the team did, what the system was for.

**What I did:**
- Specific things built, owned, fixed, or decided
- Include the unglamorous ones — migrations, debugging, on-call, cleanup

**Numbers:** anything measured. Latency, throughput, size, users, cost, error rate,
time saved, before/after. Only what was actually measured — leave blank rather than estimate.

**Hardest part:** the thing that broke, what it took to find it, what was learned.
This is where interview answers come from.

**Technologies:** languages, frameworks, tools, infrastructure — everything actually touched.

**Scope:** team size, whether it shipped, who used it, how long it ran.

## Marking each fact

Two things travel with every entry:

**Source** — where it came from: which resume, document, or conversation. Provenance matters when a claim gets questioned later.

**Fixed or embellishable** — fixed is the default and means "state this literally." Embellishable means the person is comfortable having it phrased in the best honest light. Only the person marks something embellishable; it is never inferred, and anything unmarked stays fixed.

```
- [fixed] Reduced API p99 from 340ms to 135ms  (src: 2026 resume)
- [embellishable] Owned the caching layer end to end  (src: conversation, Sep 2026)
```

This file is append-only. Add sources over time; don't overwrite or delete entries as a side effect of tailoring for one job.

## Skills

Group by category. Mark honestly:
- **Strong** — could be interviewed on it today
- **Working** — have used it, would need to review
- **Exposure** — have seen it, wouldn't claim it

The distinction matters: only "strong" and "working" belong on a resume.
